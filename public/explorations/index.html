Modernist reference and design notes

Herbert Bayer (with other artists), Film und Foto, exhibition catalogue, 1929. View the Art Institute of Chicago object record. I inspected the museum's cover image: it uses a large orange title, small aligned information, an asymmetrical plan, and generous cream space.

  • A firm alignment grid separates product and message for quick scanning.
  • A thin, muted warm rule and medium-weight sans-serif headline keep the visual hierarchy restrained.
  • Open space keeps the shirt and action easy to find.

Intended effect: make the simple class-day choice feel calm and straightforward.

Risk: the strict layout may feel too formal or institutional for a gentle Caregiver voice.

Postmodernist reference and design notes

Barbara Kruger, Untitled (Perfect), 1980. View the Art Institute of Chicago object record. I inspected the museum image: a grayscale photograph is crossed by translucent blocks and oversized type.

  • Offset paper-like panels and layered shapes suggest collage rather than a strict grid.
  • Warm coral and blue-green accents add visual tension to the same quiet message.
  • The text stays on a solid panel instead of being placed over the shirt, preserving readability.

Intended effect: let the same plain tee feel more styled and expressive without changing the promise.

Risk: the layers may feel busy and work against the low-stress mood.
